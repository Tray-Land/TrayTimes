# Tray Times

Click the tray icon for live transit departures, anywhere [Transit](https://transitapp.com) has data. Click away and the flyout closes. Hovering over the icon shows the next departures at your first three favorite stops.

- **Launching:** the first manual launch opens Settings if no stops or places have been saved. Later manual launches open the departures flyout, even if you haven't saved anything yet. Launching it again opens the running instance's window instead of starting another instance. With **Start with Windows** enabled, Windows sign-in launches stay in the tray without opening a window and don't consume the first-launch setup.
- **Favorites:** your favorite stops, with departures, route lines, and route colors. In Settings, search for a stop by name or number near the map, or browse the routes of the area's transit networks (route → direction → stop), or tap a stop on the map. You can also star a stop from the other tabs.
- **Settings:** a Mica-backed window groups **Your commute**, **Ride alerts**, and **App & data** into native cards and expanders. Favorite stops stay open when you have saved stops; otherwise **Add a stop** opens for setup. Places, API usage, and credits expand when needed. Alert, startup, and API-limit preferences apply immediately.
- **Vehicles:** click a departure row to show that route direction's live vehicles on the map. The row's own trip is highlighted with a larger yellow background, and the map frames it with its stop. A solid arrowhead shows each vehicle's heading once it has moved. Tapping a vehicle shows its label and how crowded it is, when the agency reports that. While a row stays selected its vehicles refresh every minute (only at the Normal call-budget level). Selecting a stop, tapping empty map, or showing all listed stops clears the highlight.
- **Commute:** your likely next trip. Away from home, it shows the rides that take you home; at home, the rides to work (or school, or your first custom place). Transit's trip planner picks the boarding stops and lines, transfers included, and each row says when you'd get there ("Home by 5:42 PM · 1 transfer"). If Windows location is off, it guesses from the time of day (mornings start at home). "Going to" picks a different place for now. Set your favorite places (Home, Work, School, and any custom ones) in Settings.
- **Nearby:** the lines around you, from Windows location services, each line listed once at its closest stop within about a kilometer, with route lines on the map. It dims departures that will leave before you can walk to the stop.
- **Location permission:** Commute and Nearby check access without opening a prompt. When permission is needed, use the **Grant Location Permission** banner to open the Windows permission prompt or location settings. The flyout stays open while you grant access and retries location and departures when you return; normal click-away dismissal resumes afterward. Favorites doesn't need location permission.
- **Departures:** the [Transit API v4](https://api-doc.transitapp.com/v4.html). It merges real-time and timetable departures itself: timetable-only times are labeled "Scheduled" and dimmed, late real-time departures say "4 min late", and a canceled trip is dimmed and marked "Canceled" (later canceled trips are left out of a row's strip).
- **Units and language:** distances use meters and kilometers unless Windows' region uses miles. Names and headsigns come in Windows' display language when the agency publishes one.
- **Basemap, address search, and walking times:** Azure Maps, with the map rendered by [WinUIEx.Maps](https://github.com/dotMorten/WinUIEx.Maps). Address search is biased toward your home place, not a fixed city.

The transit code sits behind `ITransitService` (`TrayTimes.Core/Transit/`). The design, the review of every Transit endpoint, and the call budget are in [docs/transit-app-migration.md](docs/transit-app-migration.md). Sign-in is a separate, unstarted phase, planned in [docs/transit-login-feasibility.md](docs/transit-login-feasibility.md).

## Project layout

- `TrayTimes/`: the WinUI 3 app. `Views/` and `Controls/` hold the UI; `Services/` holds code that needs Windows (settings storage, location, the transit cache file, tray placement).
- `TrayTimes.Core/`: plain .NET code with no WinUI or Windows dependencies. `Transit/` has the provider-neutral models, the Transit API client (`TransitApp/`), the call budget, the failure gate, the shared caches, and the request coordinators; `Models/` has the favorite stops and places and the departure snapshots; `Commute/` works out the likely next trip and turns nearby lines and trip plans into the flyout's stops.
- `TrayTimes.Tests/`: MSTest unit tests for `TrayTimes.Core` and the platform-free app services, laid out to mirror them. Run them with `dotnet test TrayTimes.Tests -c Debug -p:Platform=ARM64` (or `x64`). They use a fake HTTP handler and clock, so they need no API key, network, or package identity.
- **Request diagnostics:** the client's `Diagnostics.Observed` event and bounded `Diagnostics.Snapshot` (last 256 observations) distinguish physical request starts/completions from cache/task reuse and budget or cooldown refusals, with consumer, trigger, duration, outcome, and batch size. Debug builds also emit credential-safe `Transit request` lines to the debugger. The saved Settings tally counts every attempt, including failures.
- **Log file:** `LocalState\logs\tray-times.log` (in the package's folder under `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Packages\`) gets one line per finished Transit call (endpoint, consumer, trigger, outcome, HTTP status, time, batch size) and one line per route whose vehicle feed result changes (`vehicles route=… count=… withTrip=…`; `count=0` means the agency publishes no live positions for it). An unchanged result is logged again only after 15 minutes. The file is capped at 128 KB, then becomes `tray-times.log.old` (replacing the last one), so the log never passes about 256 KB. It never holds the key, URLs, stops, or locations.

## Setup

1. Request a Transit API key at [transitapp.com/partners/apis](https://transitapp.com/partners/apis).
2. In `TrayTimes/`, copy `OAuth.resw.sample` to `OAuth.resw` (it's gitignored), and paste the key into `TransitApiKey`. Optionally, put an Azure Maps key in `AzureMapsKey` for the basemap, address search, and walking times.
3. If your key's limits aren't Transit's free tier (5 calls a minute, 1,500 a month), set them in Settings → Transit API usage.
4. Run from `TrayTimes/` with `dotnet run -c Debug -p:Platform=ARM64` (or `x64`). Tray apps keep running after `dotnet run` returns only when launched detached: `winapp run . --detach --arch arm64`.

The first launch of this version clears favorites saved by the earlier MCTS BusTime version (their stop IDs mean nothing to Transit) and starts a new call tally. Favorite places carry over.

### Command-line builds

From the repository root, `winapp run .\TrayTimes\TrayTimes.csproj --detach` builds and launches for the current machine's architecture. `build-msix-for-gh.ps1` publishes x64 and ARM64 MSIX packages, signs them using the maintainer's local `sign-msix` function, and creates a draft release; pass `-SkipRelease` to build and sign without tagging or uploading.

Keep named controls distinct from properties used by `x:Bind` in data templates. For example, the flyout's `EmptyStateText` control must not be named `EmptyText`, which is a bound `StopGroupItem` property. That collision produces WMC1507 in Visual Studio; the standalone XAML compiler used by CLI builds instead fails with WMC9999 while loading the warning's error-message resource.

## API budget

Transit's free tier is **5 calls a minute and 1,500 a month**, about 50 a day. The quota belongs to the key, so every install sharing a key shares it. Everything below exists to stay inside it.

**The call budget.** Every request takes a slot from `TransitBudget` before it's sent. Automatic refreshes leave 2 of each minute's slots for things you do and for tracking, and those wait for a free slot instead of failing. Each day gets an even share of what's left of the month. Below 60% of today's share, the open flyout refreshes every minute. Above it, every 3 minutes. Once today's share is used, times update only when you open the flyout (if they're over 5 minutes old) or refresh. With 2% of the month left, only refreshes and tracking go through; at zero, nothing does. Settings shows the month's total, today's share, and the current level. The tally can't be reset, because the budget relies on it.

**One call per refresh.** Departures for up to 100 stops come back in one `stop_departures` call, and Favorites, Commute, Nearby, and tracking share it. A new batch waits 750 ms for other tabs' stops to join, so opening the flyout in Commute or Nearby usually costs one call for the tab and the Favorites tooltip together. A request also carries any registered browsing stop that's more than half a refresh old, so the tabs' timers settle into sharing one call a minute.

**Discovery is cached and kept.** Nearby lines (one `nearby_routes` call) are reused within 150 m for a day. Commute trip plans (one `plan` call) are reused from within 150 m of the same start, to the same place, for 3 hours. An area's route list is kept for a week, as are route details and stop searches for a day. All but the searches are saved in `LocalState\transit-cache.json`, so restarts and the flyout's one-minute teardown don't repeat them. A desktop PC that doesn't move discovers Nearby once a day.

**Route lines never cost a call.** Lines are drawn only from shapes that came with calls the app made anyway: route details, nearby lines, trip plans, and a stop's first departures each week, which ask for shapes.

**Vehicles on demand.** Positions are fetched for the selected row's route direction only, not for every listed trip.

**Tracking.** The tracked stop is checked every 3 minutes while the ride is over 10 minutes away or known only from the timetable, every 90 seconds at 5–10 minutes, then every 60 seconds. It switches to every 90 seconds once two separate checks have confirmed the ride is gone, and it slows to half that pace when the budget is tight. A timetable-only ride isn't looked for until 15 minutes before it leaves. The check at the end of the 5-minute grace costs nothing, since no answer could change the outcome. A ride tracked from 15 minutes out costs about 12 calls. Matching uses Transit's trip ID, so it also works on feeds without real-time data, including cancellations.

**Failures and cancellation.** One failure cooldown wraps every call. After a network, timeout, or server failure, timer-driven refreshes make no request for 15 s, doubling to a 5-minute cap on repeated failures; a success resets it, and requests that fail together count once. Held requests report the usual "can't reach" state and are recorded as suppressed in the diagnostics, never as empty results. Manual actions (refresh, F5, reopening the flyout, changing tab/destination/stops, picker selections, picking a row) skip that hold but are spaced at least 5 s apart after a failure. Tracking is never delayed by an ordinary cooldown. A 429, or a 503 with `Retry-After`, pauses every request for the server's `Retry-After` (60 s if absent, never more than an hour). A rejected key (HTTP 401/403) pauses timers and tracking for 30 minutes, allowing one manual retry after the 5 s spacing. A rejected query (HTTP 400/404, such as one unknown stop) never starts a cooldown. Closing the flyout or Settings cancels their in-flight requests; a shared load that another consumer still waits on keeps going.

Commute/Nearby keeps validated discovery during the flyout's one-minute warm lifetime. A quick reopen shows the last rows immediately, and routine refreshes don't wait for a new location or walking route. Location checks repeat discovery only for meaningful movement (over 25 m), changed destinations or places, or missing discovery, and the cache makes a repeat within 150 m free.
